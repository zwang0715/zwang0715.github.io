I am a K99/R00 postdoctoral fellow in the lab of Dr. Bing Ren in the Department of Genetics and Development at Columbia University Irving Medical Center. I study how gene regulatory networks control cell fate, tissue development, and regeneration. My work brings together mouse genetics and developmental biology with single-cell multiomics and functional genomics to map the regulatory genome of complex tissues.

For my Ph.D., I trained with Dr. Eric Olson at UT Southwestern Medical Center. There I used mouse models and single-cell genomics to find the gene regulatory networks behind neonatal heart regeneration, direct cardiac reprogramming, and regeneration of dystrophic skeletal muscle. As a postdoc in the Ren lab, first at UC San Diego and now at Columbia and the New York Genome Center, I led a multi-lab effort that used Paired-Tag, a single-cell method that jointly profiles histone modifications and gene expression, to build an integrative epigenomic atlas of the adult mouse brain. I have also contributed to single-cell atlases of the human brain and the failing human heart, and to new methods such as Droplet Paired-Tag and Droplet Hi-C.

Supported by an NHLBI K99/R00 Pathway to Independence Award, my current research maps the cell-type-specific gene regulatory programs and enhancers that drive cardiac maturation. To do this, I combine single-cell multiomics, deep-learning models of the regulatory genome, and human iPSC-derived cardiomyocytes. My long-term goal is to lead an independent lab that develops and applies new genomic technologies to uncover how the heart develops and regenerates, and to translate these insights into new strategies for treating heart disease.

**Research interests:** gene regulatory networks · enhancers and chromatin · single-cell multiomics · cardiac development, maturation, and regeneration · muscle biology
